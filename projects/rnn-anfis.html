Recurrent vs. neuro-fuzzy networks for weekly market direction

A business case from Euklid for the Machine Learning course at LUISS, framed as an AI systematic trading challenge: which model best predicts the direction of next week’s return, and does a loss function that cares about direction help?

Client
Euklid (business case)
Course
Machine Learning, MSc Data Science & Management, LUISS
Team
Marco Coci (lead), Dong Thi Kieu Trang, Filippo Navarra
Date
May 2024
Methods
RNN, ANFIS, custom loss, technical indicators

Technical report (PDF) Repository

Problem

For a trading signal the direction of the next move matters more than its size, yet models are usually trained on RMSE, which rewards being close in magnitude even when the sign is wrong. We compared a recurrent neural network with an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS), first trained on RMSE and then on a custom loss that adds a heavy penalty whenever the predicted direction is wrong.

Data

Six weekly OHLCV series supplied by Euklid: Amazon, IBM and Microsoft, plus the Nasdaq, S&P 500 and CAC 40 indices, from 1994 (Amazon from 1997) to March 2024, about 1,400–1,570 weeks each. The target was the weekly log return, log(Pt/Pt−1). Inputs were enriched with SMA, EMA, the stochastic oscillator, RSI and MACD. Each series was normalised and split chronologically 70 / 20 / 10 into train, validation and test.

Models

  • RNN: one recurrent layer of 20 hidden units, tanh, sequence length 10, Adam (lr 0.001), batch 32, 100 epochs.
  • ANFIS: Gaussian membership layer (10 functions over 15 inputs), T-norm rule layer and defuzzification output, with learning rates tuned per series (0.003–0.01).
  • Both used L2 weight decay (1e-5) and a step learning-rate schedule (×0.95 every 10 epochs). The direction penalty in the custom loss was 5,000 for the RNN and 5,000–10,000 for ANFIS depending on the series.

Evaluation treats the forecast as a classifier of direction: accuracy, precision, recall and F1 of up/down on the held-out weeks.

  • ANFIS was the stronger model on five of six series, and especially on the indices (0.80–0.91 accuracy, F1 up to 0.91 on the Nasdaq).
  • Amazon was the exception: the RNN did better there, which we attributed to the 2022 stock split distorting the price series that ANFIS’s membership functions were fitted on.
  • The custom loss helped selectively: it lifted the RNN by about ten points on Amazon and Microsoft and improved ANFIS on five series, but it hurt the RNN on IBM, CAC 40 and Nasdaq. Its effect depends on the series rather than being a uniform gain.

Limitations and next steps

  • Accuracies above 0.8 on weekly direction are unusually high for financial data. The natural next step is a walk-forward evaluation and a simple trading backtest with costs, to check the signal survives outside a single chronological split.
  • Structural breaks (splits, Covid) clearly affect the models; we did not add macroeconomic variables or test other architectures such as LSTMs.
